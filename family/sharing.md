The Blanket and the Pillow

My granddaughter Adaline is almost three, and she has a unique way of interpreting the world. To her, the word "share" isn't a transactional obligation; it is a declaration of presence. Since she could talk, she has used the phrase "share with Poppy" to mean that she wants to be exactly where I am, doing exactly what I am doing. If I was eating a sandwich and my hand was anywhere near her reach, she would ask, "I share with Poppy?" and take a bite.

Lately, the ritual has evolved. She decided my blanket was softer than hers, so every day at nap time, I hear the request, "Can I share your blanket?" before she piles up in front of the TV to settle in. Yesterday, the scope of the request expanded: she decided the blanket wasn't enough, and she needed my pillow, too. When her mother asked, "Hey, what are you doing with those?" 

she didn't hesitate. She yelled back as loudly as she could, "I'm sharing with Poppy!"

For her, love isn't about what I give her it's about the space she occupies alongside me.
